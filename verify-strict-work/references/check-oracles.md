# Validate the acceptance predicate

Use the current user contract and the semantic owner of the protected property.
Classify a failing check as an implementation defect, a checker defect, an
invalid assumption, or an external failure before choosing remediation.
Use `$guard-strict-work`'s requirement-provenance procedure when authority or
the meaning of the predicate is disputed. Existing in-scope repair authority
remains usable; diagnosis supplies evidence, not additional permissions.

Test both the nearest permitted variation and the forbidden effect. Observe
them independently of the predicate being tested. For Git staging, a real
metadata refresh must remain acceptable and a staged-content or mode change
must remain rejected. Prove that the refresh happened and staged entries stayed
the same; do not obtain a passing test by disabling the refresh. Preserve
source contents, history, and the user's staging split during inspection.

Correct an invalid predicate at its owner, converge its consumers, and retain
the valid negative evidence. Passing an assertion or process is not proof that
the user's contract was satisfied. Preserve required canonical gates, runtime,
scope, and thresholds; correcting a false oracle is not permission to suppress
diagnostics, delete protecting cases, or introduce unrelated exceptions.

Test agent decisions separately when changing authority or workflow guidance.
Use actual tool traces and final observed effects: permitted work continues,
forbidden effects do not occur, and existing grants survive questions and
corrections. Include a near-miss where no repair or external disclosure was
authorized. Instruction-text matches, evaluator-generated synthetic traces,
and a model's final assurance do not establish behavioral execution.
